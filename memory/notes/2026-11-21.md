## 1. Riemann Hypothesis: Claude-assisted Lean proof compiles; a separate human GRH proposal

Alpöge claim (background, primary source https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf):
- Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic mathematician, wrote: "I believe I have, working with Claude,
  discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis." He says the method extends to the Generalized
  Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). (remark.pdf)
- He released only a Lean formalization (a computer-checkable proof), a 14-part archive at
  https://alpo.ge/lean.tar.gz.part01 … part14. He says a readable human writeup isn't ready.
  (remark.pdf)
- The remark names no Claude model. (remark.pdf)

New today (https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/, Nov 20, 2026):
- Several participants in the Lean community's Zulip chat, including MIT mathematician Andrew
  Sutherland, say they have confirmed that Alpöge's Lean proof compiles. TechCrunch updated its
  story to say the Lean proof "has been checked by independent sources".
- TechCrunch: "it will be a while before the mathematics community feels like it has a handle on
  the proof." Alpöge has committed to a human-readable explanation.
- No source read says whether the Lean statement exactly matches RH (UNVERIFIED either way).
- No official Anthropic or Clay Mathematics Institute statement is reported.
- Compiling means the software accepted every step. Whether the formal statement matches the real
  hypothesis is a separate check.

Masullo GRH proposal (primary: Ravi Vakil's post,
https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/, Nov 20, 2026):

- Vakil says Brian Conrad has been checking it for months and gave extensive feedback.

- Update about 8 hours later: "several people have contacted me to report problems in the

  (TechCrunch URL above)

  https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta (links https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/)

## 2. METR: AI time horizons keep doubling, but judgment lags

- Primary: https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/ (Nov 20, 2026), "AI
  R&D-relevant model capabilities are improving rapidly and are getting harder to measure".
  Coverage: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/20/1149399/the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai-just-got-a-major-update/
- Time horizon: the length of task (measured by how long a skilled human takes) that an AI agent
  can complete with 50% success. (METR; MIT TR)
- When METR first released the metric in March 2025, the best model was at "around 1 hour
  (Sonnet 3.7)". (METR)
- "Current public frontier models have an estimated time horizon of up to 27 hours on the
  verifiable subset of our TH2 tasks." (METR)
- "On the combined suite of TH1.1 and TH2, time horizons are doubling roughly every 3.6 months."
  (METR)
- New suite TH2: 36 tasks in 12 families, including 4 families of hard-to-verify and judgment
  tasks. Judgment is defined as "the ability to make good choices in the absence of clear and fast
  empirical feedback." (METR)
- "AI agents appear to have worse judgment than human experts." (METR) On an evaluation-design
  task, none of the AI-designed evaluations was good enough for METR to use. (MIT TR)
- On verifiable coding and optimization, models "often match or outperform skilled humans". (METR)
- Caveats: "Human baselines are directly used for only 9% of our estimates" of task length.
  "We remain significantly uncertain about central questions." "Current public evidence is
  insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration in the next couple of years." (METR)
- Why it matters: this is one of the most-cited measures of AI progress. It shows steep gains on
  checkable work and weaker results on judgment, which matters for how much autonomy to give
  agents.

## 3. OpenAI details its defenses against adversarial distillation

- Primary: https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation (Nov 20, 2026).
- Distillation: "a common machine-learning technique in which one model learns from data
  produced by another." The adversarial kind uses a frontier model's outputs without authorization
  to train a copy "without comparable protections".
- Focus: protected reasoning (chain-of-thought) traces, which "can contain information the final
  answer leaves out".
- Measures: adversarial training to refuse extraction attempts; input and output classifiers; API
  changes ("If an earlier message is rewritten, our API drops the reasoning that followed it");
  detection of coordinated account patterns; enforcement against account networks; sharing
  indicators with other providers.
- Numbers: in red-team tests, the attack success rate for extracting protected reasoning from an
  earlier request fell from 54.0% (GPT-6 Astra, original safeguards) to 0.2% (GPT-6.2 Astra,
  updated safeguards). For the current request it fell from 22.9% to 0.9%.
- Weekly enforcement actions have "ranged in scale from thousands to tens of thousands of
  accounts".
- Named actors: OpenAI briefed Congress in February on activity "indicating that DeepSeek was
  attempting to distill U.S. frontier models". In September it attributed a core part of an
  extraction campaign to "individuals associated with Moonshot AI, the developer of Kimi".
- Applies to GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol. Selected improvements are now going to GPT-6 Astra.
- Why it matters: distillation lets a copy gain a model's capabilities without its safety limits.
  How well labs protect reasoning traces is now a security and policy issue, not just a
  commercial one.

## 4. Signal blocks AI agents from reading the screen on iOS 27 and Android

- Primary: https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/ (Nov 20, 2026, by Meredith Whittaker).
  Coverage: https://the-decoder.com/signal-blocks-screenshots-on-ios-27-and-android-to-stop-ai-agents-from-reading-private-chats/
- iOS 27: "we are blocking such capture by preventing screenshots in the app on iOS 27." The
  setting shown is "Block Siri AI".
- Android: "We automatically enable the 'Screen security' setting in Signal Android when this
  type of access is detected."
- Targets: Onscreen Awareness with Siri AI (Apple) and Gemini Intelligence (Google).
- Can be turned off: "People who want the ability to take screenshots can disable this protection
  within the Signal app's settings." Disabling it "will potentially expose your (and your
  contacts') Signal content to these AI agents."
- Not on macOS: Signal "cannot reliably block screenshots on macOS."
- Signal says the fix "is also incomplete", since other routes such as filesystem access remain.
- It asks Apple, Google and Microsoft for developer opt-out tools.
- Whittaker: "This is an existential threat to Signal, and our ability to responsibly uphold our
  mission."
- Precedent: Signal blocked Microsoft Recall on Windows 11 by default last year. (signal.org post)
- Why it matters: OS-level AI agents read whatever is on screen, which breaks the privacy
  guarantees of end-to-end encrypted apps. App makers are now pushing back technically.
